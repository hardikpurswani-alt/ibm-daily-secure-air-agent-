# Public Health Precautions: Particulate Exposure, Masks, and Indoor Air
Source: U.S. CDC & Environmental Public Health Guidance
URL: https://www.cdc.gov/air-quality/about/index.html
Published: 2024

## Mask & Respirator Guidance
- Cloth masks and loose surgical masks do **not** reliably filter fine PM2.5 particles (<= 2.5 µm).
- When outdoor AQI exceeds 150 (or >= 101 for sensitive individuals), a well-fitting **NIOSH-approved N95, KN95, or FFP2 respirator** significantly reduces inhaled fine particulate load when sealed cleanly over the nose and chin.
- Individuals with severe underlying respiratory or heart conditions should consult their clinician before prolonged tight-fitting respirator wear if breathing feels restricted.

## Indoor Air Protection & Clean Room Protocol
- Keep exterior windows and doors closed during elevated pollution episodes; open them selectively when verified outdoor AQI improves (often mid-afternoon when boundary-layer mixing peaks, unless ozone is high).
- Run portable air cleaners fitted with **True HEPA (H13/H14)** filters sized appropriately for the room's Clean Air Delivery Rate (CADR), avoiding ozone-generating ionizers.
- Set home/car HVAC systems to **recirculate** indoor air rather than drawing outdoor air, and install MERV-13 or higher central filters if supported by the air handler.
- Avoid indoor PM2.5 multipliers: frying/charbroiling without exhaust venting, candles, incense, wood fireplaces, and tobacco/vaping smoke.

## Medical Safety Boundary
Environmental air quality advisories support preventive daily planning only. They **never diagnose medical conditions or replace professional medical advice**. Seek urgent medical care for severe shortness of breath, chest pain, wheezing unrelieved by prescribed relievers, or dizziness.
