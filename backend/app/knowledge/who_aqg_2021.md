# WHO Global Air Quality Guidelines (2021)
Source: World Health Organization (WHO)
URL: https://www.who.int/publications/i/item/9789240034228
Published: 2021

## Recommended Air Quality Guideline (AQG) Levels
- **PM2.5 (Fine Particulate Matter):** Annual mean 5 µg/m³; 24-hour mean 15 µg/m³ (99th percentile, 3–4 exceedance days/year). Fine particles penetrate deep into lung alveoli and enter the bloodstream, driving cardiovascular and respiratory risk.
- **PM10 (Coarse Particulate Matter):** Annual mean 15 µg/m³; 24-hour mean 45 µg/m³. Road dust, construction, and agricultural debris dominate PM10 spikes.
- **Ozone (O₃):** Peak season (6-month running average of daily max 8-hour mean) 60 µg/m³; 8-hour daily maximum mean 100 µg/m³. Formed photochemically from NOx and VOCs in strong sunlight.
- **Nitrogen Dioxide (NO₂):** Annual mean 10 µg/m³; 24-hour mean 25 µg/m³. Primary marker of urban traffic exhaust and fossil fuel combustion; triggers airway inflammation.
- **Sulfur Dioxide (SO₂):** 24-hour mean 40 µg/m³. Linked to coal combustion, shipping fuel, and smelting; causes rapid bronchoconstriction in people with asthma.
- **Carbon Monoxide (CO):** 24-hour mean 4 mg/m³ (4,000 µg/m³). Impairs oxygen delivery in blood; elevated near congested intersections and poorly ventilated combustion sources.

## Health Protection Principles
WHO emphasizes there is no strictly safe threshold below which particulate matter causes zero health effects; lowering exposure at every concentration yields measurable public health benefits, especially for children, older adults, pregnant individuals, and people with asthma or heart disease.
