# US EPA Air Quality Index (AQI) & Activity Guidance (2024 Standard)
Source: U.S. Environmental Protection Agency (AirNow / 2024 PM2.5 NAAQS Revision)
URL: https://www.airnow.gov/aqi/aqi-basics/
Published: 2024

## AQI Categories & Breakpoints (PM2.5 24-hour µg/m³)
- **Good (0–50) | PM2.5 0.0–9.0 µg/m³:** Air quality is satisfactory and poses little or no risk. Ideal for outdoor exercise and open natural ventilation.
- **Moderate (51–100) | PM2.5 9.1–35.4 µg/m³:** Air quality is acceptable, though unusually sensitive individuals (people with severe asthma or respiratory reactivity) should watch for coughing or shortness of breath during prolonged outdoor exertion.
- **Unhealthy for Sensitive Groups (101–150) | PM2.5 35.5–55.4 µg/m³:** Members of sensitive groups — children, older adults, people with asthma/COPD, cardiovascular disease, and outdoor workers/athletes — should reduce prolonged or heavy outdoor exertion, take more breaks, and move intense workouts indoors.
- **Unhealthy (151–200) | PM2.5 55.5–125.4 µg/m³:** Sensitive groups should avoid prolonged or heavy outdoor exertion and consider staying indoors with filtered air. Everyone else should cut back prolonged outdoor exertion and shift workouts indoors or to lower-pollution windows.
- **Very Unhealthy (201–300) | PM2.5 125.5–225.4 µg/m³:** Health alert: risk of health effects increases for everyone. Sensitive groups should avoid all outdoor physical activity; everyone should avoid prolonged or heavy exertion and keep indoor air clean.
- **Hazardous (301–500) | PM2.5 >= 225.5 µg/m³:** Health warning of emergency conditions: everyone is more likely to be affected. Remain indoors with windows closed, run HEPA air filtration on high, set HVAC to recirculate, and wear a well-fitted N95/FFP2 respirator if essential outdoor travel is unavoidable.
